Settled design calls, newest first. Each links back to its full reasoning on the system page.

- **Splits: no random fallback allocation.** Deterministic rule instead — loyalty majority, then alternating
  draft (smaller side first). → [[Splits & Secession]]
- **Splits: negotiation is a standing proposal, not a fixed number of turns.** Resolves immediately if hidden
  claims are compatible; otherwise either side can extend ("keep talking") or force resolution ("resolve
  now"), capped at a few daily cycles. → [[Splits & Secession]]
- **Splits: two-tier structure.** Clean secession (own overwhelming-loyalty tiles, no consent needed, gives up
  contested-pool claims) vs. negotiated split (claim budgets, hidden claims revealed at cutoff, capitals
  excluded). → [[Splits & Secession]]
- **Population: simplified to one pop number + one loyalty meter per tile.** Rejected the earlier
  multi-group-per-tile / integration-meter model as too expensive to simulate and not tied to a player
  decision. → [[Population & Growth]]
- **Cut pop-level "fleeing" during splits.** Removed player agency without adding a decision; replaced by the
  loyalty-driven split mechanics above. → [[Splits & Secession]]
- **Two-layer tick cadence.** Hourly: production, movement, battles. Daily cutoff: political and economic
  decisions, resolved globally. → [[Tick Cadence]]
- **World is planets + tiles**, not a single flat map; free tiles claimed with resources, occupied tiles via
  diplomacy or war. → [[Territory & Logistics]]
- **Interplanetary expansion carries exponentially growing economic/military strain**, and that strain must be
  visible to the player, not hidden. → [[Territory & Logistics]]
- **Diplomacy (alliances, NAPs, etc.) is not game-enforced** — no mechanical block on attacking an ally.
  → [[Diplomacy]]
- **Base unit is a "collective," not a "nation."** Deliberately vague to leave room for exploring
  political-organization themes. → [[Collectives]]
- **1-hour global tick**, sci-fi setting, blending eRepublik-style economy/citizenry with NationStates-style
  diplomacy and identity. → [[Concept]]
