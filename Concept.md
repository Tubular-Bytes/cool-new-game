## Pitch

A massively multiplayer browser game with a **1-hour global tick**, inspired by play-by-email games but at a
pace that fits modern attention spans. Genre blend:

- **eRepublik** — citizens/economy as the base unit, production chains, national organization
- **NationStates** — issue-driven identity, roleplay, diplomacy without hard game enforcement
- Low fantasy setting, one big world with tiles

## Core hook

- Hourly tick resolves production, movement, and battles.
- Players don't control a "nation" from day one — they control a **[[Collectives|collective]]**, a
  deliberately vague base unit, so the game can explore political-organization themes (federation, syndicate,
  commune, bloc, etc.) rather than assuming nation-state is the only valid form.

## Why hourly, not real-time or monthly

- Monthly (play-by-email) turns are too slow for sustained engagement.
- True real-time demands attention the audience doesn't have.
- Hourly gives a habit-forming check-in rhythm (lunch break, before bed) with enough tension that decisions
  matter, without requiring the player to be online for the resolution.

See [[Tick Cadence]] for how hourly vs daily layers split responsibilities.
