## Not game-enforced #decided

Alliances, non-aggression treaties, etc. exist, but are **not mechanically enforced** — the game does not
disable the ability to attack an ally. Betrayal is always physically possible.

## Giving unenforced diplomacy texture #proposed

Raised as a risk: with nothing mechanical distinguishing an ally from a stranger, diplomacy risks living
entirely outside the game (Discord/forum agreements) and betrayal has no in-game consequence or record.
Directions floated, none locked in yet:

- **Public treaty registry** — any collective can declare a treaty; breaking one doesn't block the attack but
  logs a permanent, visible diplomatic history (reputation as data, not restriction).
- **Intelligence-sharing tied to alliance** — allies might get partial visibility into each other's territory
  or supply lines, so the alliance *does* something mechanically even though it can't prevent betrayal.
- **Consequence without prohibition** — breaking a registered treaty could carry a real cost (reputation hit
  affecting market trust, third-party tariffs, etc.) without blocking the war declaration itself.

Status: all three are proposals from early discussion, not yet evaluated against the rest of the design
(particularly [[Splits & Secession]], which already has its own escalation/conflict logic that these ideas
would need to sit alongside).

## Open

- None of the diplomacy mechanics above have been chosen over the others — this whole page is early-stage
  relative to [[Splits & Secession]] and [[Territory & Logistics]].
